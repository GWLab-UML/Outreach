# Animal Taxonomy

- Organize the tree of life with shells!
- **Target Age Group:** Teens

## Learning Objectives
- Teens will be able to draw and describe how phylogenetic trees are made
- Teens will be able to explain how phylogenetic trees inform us about the evolutionary history of organisms

## Materials 
- blank paper
- writing utensils
- shells with a lot of variation
- images of diverse taxonomic groups
- worksheet with pre-made tree and animals

## Activities 
### Introduction to Phylogenetics
1. Start with a brief introduction about evolution and phylogenetics:
    - what's evolution? how do organisms evolve? 
    - introduce the concept of tree of life
	    - what are trees? 
	    - how do we use them?
	    - why are they important or helpful?
	- can gauge their interest - phylogenetics might be too much
2. Distribute worksheet with a tree with various animals on it - work through what trait causes a branch (i.e., what makes these animals similar/different?)
3. Once everyone has a good grasp on what a tree looks like, distribute images of different animals - have them construct their own tree by drawing lines to connect the pictures
4. Have each person share their tree - is everyone's the same? 

### Shell Taxonomy
1. Each student will be given a shell pile of various shapes, colors, textures and yarn/rope.
2. Give the students time to construct their own phylogenetic tree with the shells in front of them. Walk around and ask the students questions about their trees.
3. Pause and regroup - instruct students to take a look at their neighbors' trees - are they all the same? Are they different? 
	- Talk through the different ways you can construct trees - maybe someone focused on texture vs. color vs. shape!
4. If time remains, repeat with pictures of different organisms.
