# Tragedy of the Watershed

- In this 40 minute activity, kids act as land developers along the Merrimack River looking to build some infrastructure. However, building comes at a cost. Throughout the activity, kids learn how upstream pollution leads to larger downstream problems, and how we can make better choices to reduce and prevent pollution.
- **Target Age Group:** 10-14 (but can be scaled in different ways to make easier or more challenging)

## Learning Objectives
- Describe why some buildings polluted more than others
- Understand that upstream areas all contribute to the same watershed - our choices have consequences and it takes collaboration to reduce or clean up pollution
- Understand that their actions polluting the watershed will also affect the animals and plants, not just humans
- Give examples of environmental impact and clean-up efforts in the Merrimack River

## Materials 
- Colored legos (we've also done this with play-doh and that works too!)
- Pollutants
	- Food coloring
	- Aquarium rocks
	- Larger rocks
	- Oil 
- Scoops for pollutants (tsp/tbsp)
- Dropper for oil
- Water containers
	- 1 small for each group/individual 
	- 1 large for everyone - labeled “Merrimack River” 
- Laminated [key for lego block colors and corresponding pollutant](https://github.com/GWLab-UML/Outreach/blob/main/Activities/Tragedy%20of%20the%20Watershed/lego_colorKey.pdf)
- Expo markers
- Laminated [map of the Merrimack River](https://github.com/GWLab-UML/Outreach/blob/main/Activities/Tragedy%20of%20the%20Watershed/Merrimack-River-Map.jpg) 
- Water

## Activities 
1. Introduce the Merrimack Watershed and the scenario: each group is going to construct a building (house, farm, factory, store, etc) along the river
2. Have kids mark where they want to build on the laminated map, then seat them in order from upstream to downstream 
3. Each group is given a set of legos, pollutants, and a laminated key; explain that they should use tick marks to keep track of how many of each color lego they use
	- Might be good to do a quick example with a few blocks, if the group is younger
	- *Optional*: could designate roles (builder, engineer, EPA, etc) 
4. Give them ~10 minutes to build and tally
	- *Optional*: switch roles after ~5 minutes
5. While they’re building, give each group a small tub of water
6. After building time is over, groups will add pollutants that correspond to the legos they used
7. Once all pollutants have been added, pause here and have them show their finished building and make observations about the type and amount of pollution they contributed to the whole group
8. Encourage them to get up and walk around to see and compare the pollution created by other groups
9. Starting upstream, each group will add their polluted water one at a time to the larger bin labeled “Merrimack River” 
10. *Optional*: Add fishing activity. After each group’s addition to the river, throw in some fish (variable for each group, depending on if they used any materials that were toxic to fish) - see how much harder to fish it gets as the river gets more polluted
11. Once the last group has added their polluted water, everyone can observe the “Merrimack River” 
	- How is the downstream water different from the upstream water? 
	- Can you see which group added the most (no because it’s all mixed - and that’s one of the problems with water policy)
	- What could you do to clean this up? (big picture - work together since it’s a shared resource)
	- How can we reduce and/or prevent pollution? (use more eco-friendly building materials, develop new materials that might incorporate pollutants to take them out of the environment, introduce policy to regulate building materials, etc)
	- How does this relate to the real Merrimack River? (Used to be very polluted, people are still working together to clean it up)
