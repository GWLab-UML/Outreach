# Oyster anatomy and dissection
- Students will start by collecting data on their oyster (measuring length and width, observing the outside) and then identifying and dissecting tissues from the shucked oyster. They will put tissues in corresponding tubes to "sample" the oyster.
- **Target Age Group:** 9-12 but can be modified for younger groups

## Learning Objectives
- become familiar with oysters - identify the different tissues and parts of an oyster (mantle, gill, muscle at the very least)
- become familiar with using microscopes


## Materials
- live oysters 
- shucking knives
- shucking gloves
- lab gloves 
- cafeteria trays
- weigh boats
- tube racks
- non-sterile 1.5-2 mL tubes
- squirt bottle with water
- rags/paper towels and disposable bags (for cleanup)
- forceps (not the good ones)
- blunt scalpels or scissors
- stereoscope
- [oyster anatomy worksheet](https://github.com/GWLab-UML/Outreach/blob/main/Activities/OysterAnatomy/OysterAnatomy_worksheet.pdf)
- rulers/calipers 
- fine-tipped sharpies
- pencils/writing utensils

## Activity
>depending on the number of students, work individually or in partners/small groups
1.	Intoduce oysters
    - Do you eat oysters? Where do they live? Are they an animal?
2.	Hand out oysters. BEFORE SHUCKING have students observe the outside.
    - Are there other animals living on the shell? Do both sides of the shell look the same? 
    - If you need more time, you can also have groups/students switch oysters - in what ways are the two oysters different?
3. Hand out calipers and rulers. Have students collect measurements (length/width) using both tools. Have groups compare their data.
    - Which tool is more accurate? 
    - Make sure students are recording down units! Can talk about the importance of units when trying to figure out which group has the biggest/smallest oyster
4. Shuck oysters. Have students observe the tissues inside and try to label the anatomy on the worksheet. 
    - Walk around and point out different tissues. 
    - Can also look at the shell - are there signs of infection? (blister worms, new shell growth)
5. With dissecting tools, have students dissect different tissues, placing them in tubes filled with water. Have students label the tubes based on what tissue they put in there. 
    - Talk about how this is what we do to sample and study oysters!
6. If time remains, students can look at different tissues using the stereoscope
    - Do the tissues look the same? Is there anything you can see with the scope that you couldn't see before?
