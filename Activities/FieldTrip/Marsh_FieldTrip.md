## Salt Marsh Activity Stations
### Snail Hunt
1. Organize into two groups of 3-4 people. Roles:
    - Snail collector (need gloves, quadrat, plastic jar)
    - Snail measurer (2) (need caliper or rulers)
    - Data collector (need transect, [data sheet](https://github.com/GWLab-UML/Outreach/tree/main/Activities/FieldTrip/Outreach_snail_datasheet.pdf), and pencil)
2. Spread out along a transect using a quadrat
3. Set up transects in different enviornments (i.e., patens vs. alterniflora)
4. Set quadrat every 1 meter
5. Cut back hay with knife
6. If lots of snails, collect 10 per quadrat

### Oyster Farming and Wild Reefs
1. Look at the oyster farm - point out the different parts of the farm, how farms work and who they supply
2. Talk about intertidal vs. subtidal environments
3. Point out the wild oyster bed nearby - pull some oysters out!
4. Have each person hold a clump of oysters - observe how they look different from farmed oysters (shape, size, color, etc.)
    - can talk about how oysters cement themselves to rocks/other oysters
    - explain why we can't eat the wild oysters
5. Open an oyster up - does it look like how you expected it to?
    - point out tissue types - especially gonad if they're reproductive
    - look for new shell growth - point out signs of mud blisters or boring sponge

### Birding
1. Walk away from the commotion from the other stations
2. Use binoculars to try to find different common salt marsh birds with the [bird field guide](https://github.com/GWLab-UML/Outreach/tree/main/Activities/FieldTrip/saltmarsh_birds.pdf)
    - Red winged black bird
    - Tree swallow
    - Double crested cormorant
    - Least terns
    - Eastern willet
    - Egret
    - Osprey
    - Saltmarsh sparrow (endangered!)

## Tidepooling
This part of the field trip can be dependent on the vibe of the group - we had a plan but decided to go with a less structured exploration of the beach since the teens were excited to be on the beach and in the water. 

We walked around in the water with the teens and gave them [intertidal field guides](https://github.com/GWLab-UML/Outreach/tree/main/Activities/FieldTrip/Intertidal_fieldGuide.pdf) so they could ID what they were finding. We pointed them in the direction of mussels/crabs/snails/etc. They were really interested in the crabs, so we instructed them on how to hold them and how to ID and sex them. We also brought out the refractometer and pH strips so they could test the water chemistry. 

If more structure is wanted, you could lay a transect along the tidepool and lay quadrats every meter to record what is there in the provided [data sheet](https://github.com/GWLab-UML/Outreach/tree/main/Activities/FieldTrip/PI_outreach_datasheet.pdf).
